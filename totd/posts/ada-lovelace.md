radx.io the codable math game® is exactly that: codable. Because we are just playing with on-off bits, we can make those bits mean anything. Converting from a binary bit to another number base is done following certain rules. As an entry point for players ages 10+ we decided to stick to a fun, fast-paced binary-to-decimal variant that we call Ada!

**Ada!** is named in honor of **Ada Lovelace (1815–1852)**, often regarded as the first computer programmer. Lovelace worked with Charles Babbage on his proposed *Analytical Engine*, a mechanical general-purpose computing machine conceived in the 19th century. In her notes on the Engine, Ada described a method for calculating Bernoulli numbers: an algorithm written for a machine that had not yet been built. For this reason, her work is widely recognized as the first published computer program.

Lovelace’s contribution is more about how she thought than what code she wrote. She understood computation as a process of symbolic manipulation governed by rules, distinct from the physical machine itself. Naming our first game **Ada!** is both a tribute and a reminder that before modern computers, people were already learning to think computationally.
